Static
Testing

How to detect defects without executing software, review work products, and improve quality from the earliest stages.

Key idea

Reading, reviewing, and analyzing are also forms of testing. Many defects can be found before the system is executed.

3.1.1 · Work productsWhat can be reviewed

Almost any work product can be examined

Simple rule: if a person can read and understand it, it can usually be reviewed.

01

Requirements

Specifications, user stories, and acceptance criteria.

02

Code

Source code, rules, conventions, and internal structure.

03

Testware

Test plans, test cases, and test charters.

04

Documentation

Models, contracts, backlog items, and project documentation.

For automation: the product needs a formal structure or syntax that a tool can analyze.
Reviewing is not limited to codeWork products

3.1.3 · ComparisonStatic / Dynamic

Two complementary ways to find problems

Static testing
Direct
defect
  • Does not execute the software.
  • Works with non-executable products.
  • Finds defects directly.
  • Evaluates maintainability, consistency, and structure.
VS
Dynamic testing
Observable
failure
  • Requires executing the software.
  • Observes actual behavior.
  • Triggers failures and investigates causes.
  • Measures runtime behavior and performance.
Static = reviewDynamic = execute
3.1.3 · ExamplesTypical defects

Problems a review can discover before execution

?

Requirements

Is it clear?

Ambiguity, contradictions, duplicates, incomplete information, or non-testable criteria.

Example: “the system must be fast” without a measurable target.
◇

Design

Is it well structured?

Poor modularization, inefficient data structures, or poorly defined interfaces.

Example: incompatible parameters between modules.
</>

Code

Are there internal problems?

Uninitialized variables, duplicated or unreachable code, excessive complexity.

Example: a branch that can never be executed.
!

Standards & security

Does it follow the rules?

Convention deviations, specific vulnerabilities, or structural noncompliance.

Example: buffer overflow detectable through analysis.

3.2.2 · Process05 activities

An effective review follows a clear process

01

Planning

Scope, purpose, criteria, effort, and schedule.

02

Initiation

People, roles, access, and information are prepared.

03

Individual review

Each reviewer identifies anomalies, questions, and recommendations.

04

Analysis

Findings, owners, status, and actions are discussed.

05

Fix & report

Defects are fixed, followed up, and results are reported.

Important: an anomaly is not always a defect; it must first be analyzed.
Plan · Review · Analyze · FixISO/IEC 20246
3.2.3 · RolesResponsibilities

Each role contributes something different

Manager

Provides resources

Decides what is reviewed and allocates people, time, and resources.

Author

Creates and fixes

Is responsible for the work product being reviewed.

Moderator

Facilitates

Manages the meeting, time, mediation, and a safe environment.

Scribe

Records

Documents anomalies, decisions, and new findings.

Reviewer

Examines

Evaluates the work product and identifies questions and anomalies.

Review leader

Organizes

Takes overall responsibility and organizes participants, date, and location.

One person may perform several rolesReview team
3.2.4 · TypesFrom informal to formal

Not all reviews need the same level of formality

1

Informal review

The lightest

No defined process or formal documented output is required.

Objective: detect anomalies quickly.
2

Walkthrough

Led by the author

Can generate ideas, consensus, learning, and detect anomalies.

Key: prior individual review may be optional.
3

Technical review

Technical decisions

Performed by technically qualified reviewers and led by a moderator.

Objective: consensus on technical issues.
4

Inspection

The most formal

Follows the complete process and collects metrics for improvement.

Objective: find the maximum number of anomalies.
Informal → Walkthrough → Technical → InspectionIncreasing formality
3.2.5 · SuccessClosing

A good review needs more than a meeting

01

Clear objectives

Define purpose and measurable exit criteria.

02

Preparation

Provide enough time and review small sections.

03

People & culture

Training, management support, feedback, and learning.

04

Facilitation

Choose the right review type and conduct meetings effectively.

Reviewing early prevents a small problem from becoming an expensive defect.
